# Studio

The trust page. A prospect in London or Dubai arrives here to answer one
question: *are these people real, and are they good?*

Answer it with specifics. Vagueness on this page is what makes a small studio
look like a front.

---

## Hero

> **Eyebrow** — Studio

> # Small on purpose.

> Girgit Labs is a design studio working from India for clients mostly
> everywhere else. Small enough that the person you brief is the person who

**Notes.** "Working from India for clients mostly everywhere else" states the
fact in eight words with no defensiveness and no explanation. Never expand it.
The moment you justify being in India, you've conceded there's something to
justify.

## Section 1 — How we work

> ## <span class="hi" data-en="stubbornness">ज़िद</span> about the right things
>
> **Expertise decides, not seniority.** Whoever knows the most about the
> problem in front of us leads that decision. Every project has one owner who
> keeps it moving and is accountable for the date — that's coordination, not
> rank.
>
> **We say things early.** If something is going wrong, you hear it the day we
> know, not the day it becomes your problem. A studio that only ever reports
> good news is a studio managing you.
>
> **We argue.** Internally, about each other's work, directly. And with you,
> when a decision would visibly damage the result. You still decide. But you're
> paying for a point of view, and a studio that agrees with everything is
> giving you a very expensive pair of hands.
>
> **Nothing ships unreviewed.** Every deliverable is looked at by someone who
> didn't make it.

**Notes.** ज़िद — stubbornness — is the page's one Hindi word, and it's the
right one: it's affectionate in Hindi, closer to "principled obstinacy" than
plain pig-headedness, and the section explains itself immediately. The heading
reads fine as *"Stubbornness about the right things"* for anyone who never
hovers.

## Section 4 — Where we are

> ## India, and wherever you are
>
> `[[City, India]]`. We work with clients in the Gulf, the UK, Europe and North
> America, and we've never met most of them in person.
>
> The distance is usually an advantage. Work lands overnight and you review it
> with your morning coffee. For the Gulf we overlap almost the whole day; for
> the UK, most of the afternoon; for the US, we hand over as you're starting.
>
> Calls are `[[Google Meet / Zoom]]`. Files come to you the same day they're
> made. Nobody has ever needed to be in the same room as us to get a website.

**Notes.** Same rule as everywhere: timezone as an advantage, never price as an
advantage. This section should never contain the words "cost," "affordable,"
"value for money" or "rates." The operating doc's positioning depends on it.

## Section 5 — AI

One short section, on the studio page, where a curious client can find it. Not
on the homepage.

> ## On AI
>
> We use it heavily — research, production, QA, the repetitive parts of
> frontend. It's part of why a small team ships at this pace.
>
> It doesn't design the work and it doesn't decide anything. Nothing reaches
> you that a person didn't make, review and stand behind.
>
> We'd rather you noticed the speed and the quality than the tooling.

**Notes.** This is the operating doc's AI position translated exactly: a real
capability, demonstrated through outcomes, never the loudest claim. The last
line closes the subject politely so the page can move on.

Do not remove this section. AI-avoidant silence in 2026 reads as either
dishonesty or obsolescence, and a client who suspects you're hiding it will ask
on a call — better they read your answer first.
