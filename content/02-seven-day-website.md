# The Seven-Day Website

The single most important page on the site. Everything the homepage promises in
four sentences gets defended here in full. Written to survive a sceptical
reader — someone who has been burned by a fast agency before and is looking for
the catch.

Tell them the catch. It's the fastest way to be believed.

## Section 1 — The honest version

> ## What "seven days" actually means
>
> It means seven days of building. It does not mean seven days from the first
> email.
>
> Before the clock starts we do the slow work: understand what you're actually
> trying to do, collect your content and assets, study your references and the
> competitors who matter, agree a sitemap, set a visual direction, and write
> down the scope so precisely that neither of us can be surprised by it later.
>
> That groundwork takes as long as it takes — usually a few days, sometimes
> longer if content isn't ready. It is not billed as a separate phase and it is
> not optional. It is the reason seven days is possible at all.
>
> Agencies that promise a week from the first email are either shipping a
> template or about to miss the date.

**Notes.** Naming the competitor's failure mode in the last line is the strongest
sentence on the page. It converts the obvious objection — "nobody builds a good
site in a week" — into agreement with you.

## Section 2 — What you bring

Published deliberately. It defends the promise, it qualifies leads before they
reach a call, and it reads as professional rather than restrictive.

> ## What we need from you before day one
>
> **Your objective.** What the site is for, and how you'll know it worked.
>
> **Your content.** Copy, or the raw material for it. If you don't have copy, we
> can write it — that's additional scope and it adds time, and we'd rather tell
> you now than on day four.
>
> **Your logo and brand identity.** Required. The seven-day process assumes a
> brand already exists to build on. If it doesn't, we can create one first — a
> separate piece of work with its own timeline.
>
> **Reference sites.** Three to five. Include one you dislike; it tells us more
> than the five you love.
>
> **Your assets.** Photography, product shots, video, logos, fonts, whatever
> exists.
>
> **One decision-maker.** One person with the authority to approve. Design by
> committee and seven days becomes seven weeks — that's not a policy, it's
> arithmetic.

**Notes.** "Include one you dislike" is a real technique and it signals craft.
"One decision-maker" is the most commercially valuable line on the page — it
prevents the single failure mode most likely to break the promise.

## Section 3 — The seven days

> ## How the week runs

> **Day 1 — Direction**
> You see real screens, in a browser, not a deck. The homepage above the fold,
> the type, the colour, the way it moves. You approve the direction or we turn
> it. Turning on day 1 costs a day. Turning on day 5 costs the week.
>
> **Days 2–3 — Build**
> Full design across the agreed pages, built as we go. Not mockups handed to a
> developer — the designer is writing the frontend.
>
> **Day 3/4 — Checkpoint**
> The real site, on your phone, that you can click through. This is where you
> give your revision notes. One round is included.
>
> **Days 5–6 — Revision and finish**
> Your notes, then the parts nobody notices until they're missing: responsive
> behaviour down to small phones, empty and error states, meta tags and social
> cards, page speed, accessibility basics, forms that actually deliver.
>
> **Day 7 — Live**
> Deployed to your domain, or handed over as clean code if you'd rather deploy
> it yourself. You get the files either way.

## Section 4 — What V1 means

Directly counters the assumption that fast means rough.

> ## <span class="hi" data-en="unfinished">कच्चा</span> is not what we ship
>
> A V1 from us is not a minimum viable anything. It's a finished website that
> happens to be the first one — <span class="hi" data-en="solid">पक्का</span>,
> settled, complete for the scope it covers.
>
> If what you've asked for genuinely cannot be done well in seven days, we don't
> compress it and we don't pad the timeline. We tell you before you pay, agree
> the strongest complete thing that does fit in seven days, ship that, and move
> the rest to Phase 2.
>
> You end up live in a week either way. That's the point.

**Notes.** Two Hindi words in one section is above budget, but they're a
deliberate pair — कच्चा / पक्का, raw and set — and each is translated by the
sentence it sits in. Don't add a third to this page beyond the hero.

## Section 5 — Scope and revisions

The boring section. Put it in anyway; the clients worth having read it.

> ## Scope, revisions, and what happens when things change
>
> **The seven days apply to the agreed scope.** Not to a scope that grows on day
> four. New requirements after sign-off are new work with their own quote and
> timeline — we'll always tell you the cost before we do it.
>
> **One revision round is included.** A second may be included depending on the
> package. A revision is refinement of what was agreed, not a change of
> direction — a change of direction is new scope, and we'll say so at the time
> rather than absorbing it silently and resenting it.
>
> **We'll push back.** If you ask for something that would visibly hurt the
> work, we'll explain why and propose an alternative. You still decide. But we
> reserve the right to decline a decision that would make the result something
> we wouldn't put our name on.
>
> **You own it on final payment.** Full IP transfer, source files, code.

>

> **Design + frontend + deployment** — live on your domain.

## Section 8 — Objections

Written as an FAQ because that's what it is. Answer the hard ones honestly; the
soft ones aren't worth the space.

> **How can seven days be any good?**
> Because a small senior team with no handoffs and no queue moves at a
> completely different speed to a large agency, and because all the deciding
> happens before day one. The week is spent building, not aligning.
>
> **Is it a template?**
> No. Every site is designed for the brief. If a template were the answer,
> you wouldn't need us — you'd need a subscription.
>
> **What if I miss a checkpoint?**
> The clock pauses. We're not going to hold you to a date you couldn't make;
> we'll agree a new one.
>
> **What if I hate the day 1 direction?**
> Say so on day 1. One turn at that stage costs a day and we've built for it.
> That is exactly why the checkpoint exists.
>
> **Do you work in my timezone?**
> We're in India, which means work usually lands while you're asleep and you
> review it with your morning coffee. For the Gulf we overlap most of the day;
> for the UK, half; for the US, we hand over as you start.
>
> **What happens after day 7?**
> Usually Phase 2, or the next thing entirely. Most of our work is repeat work.
> `[[Confirm before publishing this line.]]`

**Notes.** The timezone answer turns the most common objection to hiring in
India into an advantage without ever mentioning cost. Never pair it with a
price comparison — that's the trap the operating doc warns about.
