## Decisions this is built on

- **Positioning** — "We don't have a house style. We have yours," with the
  seven-day speed in the sub. Adaptation carries the brand; speed carries the
  conversion.
- **The name is reclaimed, not avoided.** *Girgit* is an insult in Hindi. The
  homepage says so, then turns it into the studio's whole argument. This is the
  fold people will screenshot.
- **Hindi appears as inline Devanagari words that reveal their English on
  hover.** Two to four per page, never load-bearing — delete any one of them and
  the sentence still reads. That rule protects the Gulf, UK and US readers who
  are priority one.
- **Reference weighting** — Sparklin for voice, Litmus for single-metaphor
  discipline, Wolff Olins for restraint on project lines. SandCup as the
  counter-example.
- **Speed is explained without AI.** Small senior team, no handoffs, groundwork
  finished before the clock starts. All true, all sayable.
- **India is a fact and a timezone advantage, never a discount.**

## Open items

Three things need a decision before build, and one is a genuine fork:

1. **Price on the seven-day page.** The earlier assumption was no price. Having
   written the page I'd revisit it — a single floor ("typically start at $X")
   filters bargain hunters and supports premium positioning better than silence.
   Argument is in `02-seven-day-website.md`, Section 7. Layout works either way.
2. **Team photographs.** Faces are the evidence a small international studio
   exists. If nobody wants to be photographed, say so and the Studio page gets
   rewritten — it'll be weaker.
3. **Real project details** — client names, one-line outcomes, and at least one
   real metric per case study. Templates and worked examples are in
   `03-work.md`; the writing rules there matter more than the blanks.

## Before anything ships

Grep for `[[` — nothing should remain. Then have every Devanagari string checked
by a native reader; nuqta marks (ज़ फ़ क़ ग़) are the usual failure point and
they change the word. A typo in the one Hindi word on a page is more visible
than a typo anywhere else on the site.

Full checklist at the end of `07-global.md`.
